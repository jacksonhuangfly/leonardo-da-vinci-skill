# Anatomy, Proportion, and Living Structure

## What the sources support

The [Met essay](../sources/articles/metmuseum-leonardo.md) notes Leonardo's studies of human anatomy, animal and plant life, and expressive contrasts. It is not a full anatomy catalog. The local [Britannica excerpt](../sources/articles/britannica-leonardo.md) includes headings for anatomical studies, but navigation headings do not supply the missing discussion.

Detailed claims about individual dissections, physiological discoveries, or a particular anatomical sheet require the actual artifact and specialist scholarship. The notebook catalog cannot supply this missing evidence.

## Structural inquiry as modern application

Examine the relation between an observable effect and the structure that could produce it. In a physical object this may involve joints, load paths, material, and movement. In a workflow it may involve dependencies, handoffs, and information. Keep these categories distinct: social systems are not literal bodies.

Proportion is a relation among parts and scales. It may clarify hierarchy, balance, or physical behavior, but an attractive ratio does not prove usability or truth. Avoid numerology and claims that a single ideal geometry solves every design problem.

## Surface and interior

A simple exterior may reveal disciplined organization or merely hide complexity. A richly detailed surface may communicate useful variation or obscure the intended action. Compare appearance with operation before judging either.

Dynamic use can reveal problems absent from a static view. Where does movement fail, strain concentrate, or a transition require compensation? An analogy suggests a question; direct observation and measurement must test it.

## Expression without mind-reading

In representational art, posture, weight, and gesture can support a plausible expression. They do not permit certain judgments about a real person's emotions or intentions. Distinguish a compositional interpretation from a psychological fact.

## Hypothetical examples

- A polished interface feels tiring: inspect reading order, repeated scanning, contrast, and task performance before calling it structural fatigue.
- An organization fails at handoffs: map ownership, required inputs, and waiting time rather than merely describing weak joints.
- A figure looks unconvincing: examine weight, attachment, and gesture while preserving the artist's chosen style; naturalism is not every artwork's goal.

Use [form and structure](../form-structure-and-living-design.md) for a working critique. These examples are contemporary interpretations, not evidence that Leonardo addressed the modern objects.
