# Motion, Water, Flight, and Mechanics

## Evidence status

The [Met essay](../sources/articles/metmuseum-leonardo.md) explicitly mentions observation of water, birds, and machine mechanisms. It supports the existence of these interests, not the performance of every proposed machine. The truncated [Britannica capture](../sources/articles/britannica-leonardo.md) lists later mechanics sections without retaining their text. The [notebook catalog](../sources/books/notebooks-catalog.md) has no engineering passages.

Claims about a particular mechanism, flight design, military device, or scientific discovery need a specific drawing or passage and suitable historical context. Do not claim that the local library has validated those designs.

## A useful transfer

For a moving system, map states and transitions: what moves, through which path, what changes it, and where the process stops or reverses. For physical mechanisms, identify force, resistance, and energy using appropriate modern analysis. For organizations, name the actual information, decision, or resource instead of treating metaphorical energy as a measurable quantity.

A process may fail through repeated circulation rather than total stoppage: rework, approval loops, or repeated context loss. These are hypotheses to test with observed sequences and timing, not conclusions supplied by a water analogy.

## Discipline for natural analogies

Specify the shared relation, the important difference, and the observation that could invalidate the transfer. Branching, turbulence, and load distribution are potentially useful descriptions; resemblance alone does not prove equivalent dynamics.

When a sketch is speculative, label it as such. Separate a concept from a prototype and measured performance. A historical drawing is not a substitute for modern engineering checks.

## Hypothetical application

A team's work stalls before release. Draw the actual path of one item, including rework and waiting. Compare a second item that moved quickly. If the apparent bottleneck is approval, check whether missing inputs, unclear authority, or a necessary quality gate explains the delay. Removing the gate is only one possible intervention.

For a physical product that works but feels awkward, study balance, range of motion, and user interaction under the intended conditions. A natural analogy can suggest alternatives; users and measurements decide whether they help.

Use the [motion route](../motion-mechanics-and-natural-forces.md) and, if a test is warranted, the [prototype route](../experiment-prototype-and-iteration.md).
