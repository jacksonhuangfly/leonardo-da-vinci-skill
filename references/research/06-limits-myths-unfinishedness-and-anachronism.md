# Limits, Myths, Unfinishedness, and Anachronism

## Historical interpretation versus contemporary use

This repository proposes ways to apply observation, drawing, structural inquiry, and experimentation. It does not recover Leonardo's private mind or establish his endorsement of AI, software, startups, or a current aesthetic trend. Label an application as contemporary interpretation; never put newly written advice in a historical speaker's mouth.

The [source inventory](../sources/README.md) contains two secondary article captures and a catalog record, not a complete primary manuscript corpus. A catalog's automatically generated summary is not Leonardo's text. A section title in a truncated capture does not establish its unseen content.

## Distinctions that prevent overclaiming

| Claim being considered | Required distinction |
|---|---|
| An invention was successful | A drawing, a proposal, construction, and measured performance are different. |
| Unfinishedness proves creativity | An open study, technical obstacle, interrupted commission, and abandoned task have different causes. |
| Range makes expertise unnecessary | Transferable methods can travel; domain knowledge and verification still matter. |
| A visual solution is correct | A compelling representation may conceal a false assumption. |
| A work belongs to Leonardo | Attribution, workshop participation, dating, and edition need suitable evidence. |
| A modern technology was predicted | An analogy is not historical foresight or endorsement. |

## Interpreting unfinishedness

Ask what was learned, what was promised, what changed, and what is still required. For a historical work, missing records may prevent a confident causal explanation. For the user's current project, preserve deadlines and obligations rather than importing a romance of unfinished genius.

Exploration can be productive when it improves observation, exposes a mechanism, or rules out alternatives. It becomes less defensible when no new information is gained and the cost of delay grows. Define completion according to the user's purpose; not every notebook study must become a product.

## Source gaps

Specific claims about mirror writing, private motives, library inventories, individual codices, and transmission through named pupils need further sources. Earlier unsourced discussions of these topics should not be treated as verified research.

The [Met essay](../sources/articles/metmuseum-leonardo.md) can anchor drawing and compositional discussion; the [Britannica excerpt](../sources/articles/britannica-leonardo.md) can anchor selected biography and observation themes. Neither licenses all modern transfer claims.

## Repairing a hypothetical modern answer

If a user says that Leonardo proves endless iteration is best, ask what the iterations reveal and what finishing is meant to accomplish. If a user says he would obviously love generative tools, compare how the tool affects observation, externalization, and judgment without inventing his opinion. If the user deliberately wants playful fiction, clearly frame it as fiction and preserve the requested creative scope.
